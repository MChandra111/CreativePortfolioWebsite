
LLMs are a specific **class** of [[AI]] systems trained on massive amounts of text data.

They understand, generate, and manipulate human language in a way that mimics human-like responses. They're built using deep learning techniques, like neural networks, and use **billions** or parameters. This provides context, semantics, and nuances of language.

### **Core characteristics**

Scale - Enormous Dataset (untrainable on your own)

Versatility - Capable of translation, summarization, question answering, coding, and creative writing

Contextual Awareness - Use mechanisms like "self-attention" in transformer architectures to weigh the importance of words in context

---

### **How they work (process)**

1) Data collection & preprocessing -> Gather and clean vast text corpora (books, websites)
2) [[Tokenization]] - Break text into words (words, subwords, or characters)
3) [[Embeddings]] - Convert tokens into numerical vectors (embeddings) that capture meaning in [[Context]]
4) Self-Attention - Transformer models analyze **relationships between words** in a sentence
5) Training - Predict the next word in a sequence. Supervised learning. Adjust parameters (fine-tuning) to minimize errors
6) Deployment - Fine-tuned or adapted for specific tasks

---

### **Challenges & Considerations**

Bias - 50% of all data on the internet is in the English language

Computation Cost - Training and running large models requires **A LOT** of resources

Evaluation - Ongoing research into benchmarks and transparency
